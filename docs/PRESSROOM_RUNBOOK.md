# The Pressroom Runbook

**For Ben and David. Plain words, big type, printable.**
One page a morning, three buttons, and nothing here ever requires
remembering a password before coffee.
New to the store? Read **`docs/HOW_THE_SHOP_WORKS.md`** first — the
two-page tour of the whole operation. This page is just the buttons.

---

## The morning (five minutes)

1. Open **the pressroom page**: `thomasbroadside.co/pressroom` on the
   shop tablet or phone. First time only: it asks for the shop token —
   it's on the card taped inside the supply cabinet. It remembers after
   that.
   *Prefer paper?* Jacob's clerks print the same thing:
   **`pressroom/RUN_SHEET.md`** — ask Jacob to leave it on the bench.
2. Read the queue. Oldest orders first. Editions are marked **EDITION —
   NUMBER & INSPECT** and wait for David.
3. That's it. Print what's queued, when the press day allows. The store
   never promises a date you didn't give it.

## The three buttons

Each order card has one button at a time:

- **START** → tells the store it's on the press. Tap when you pull stock.
- **SHIPPED** → tap ONLY with the tube sealed, labeled, and the tracking
  number typed in the little box. This sends the customer their "it's on
  the way" note, so it has to be true.
- **HOLD** → problem with the order (address, stock, damage). Type one
  short line about why. Jacob's clerks will chase it and come back to you.

Nobody but the two of you (and Jacob) can press these. The machines are
**not allowed** to press SHIPPED — that's yours alone.

## The standard (David's page)

Museum-grade is the back of the fence: the parts nobody sees.

- Inspect **every sheet** front and back: impression, ink coverage,
  square trim, clean margins. A crooked sheet costs the story, not just
  the sale — pull it, say so, reprint it.
- **Editions:** number in pencil, bottom left, `N of 250`; emboss the
  maker's mark; log the number on the ticket. You sign the ticket's
  INSPECTED box. No signature, no shipping — and no one can overrule
  that, including Jacob. It's written into the company rules [D6].
- **Packing:** clean hands or gloves, sheet rolled loose around the
  core, kraft wrap, end caps taped flat, label straight, the colophon
  card in every tube. The inside of the tube is part of the product.

## Stock (Ben's page)

- Once a week, count: sheets per design, tubes, cotton, labels. Text the
  counts to Jacob or say them to the clerk — they keep the book. Never
  let the book guess.
- The book flags any design under **50 sheets** for reprint. You and
  Jacob pick the run; the press wants batches of 250–500, so runs get
  bundled.
- Supplies: you buy, the book records. A late tube costs more than a
  sale.

## When something's wrong

| Problem | Do this |
|---|---|
| Damaged in the mail | HOLD + one line. Shop desk letters the customer; we reprint first, refund second. |
| Address looks wrong | HOLD + one line. Desk confirms with the customer. |
| Out of stock for an order | HOLD + one line. It joins the next run; desk letters the customer honestly. |
| Site or page won't load | Call Jacob. The press doesn't stop; paper run sheet still works. |
| A sheet isn't right | David pulls it. No discussion needed, ever. |

## What the machines do at night (so you know)

Orders that were paid become tickets by morning. The run sheet is fresh
by 7. The customer letters, the pins, the catalog pages — Jacob's clerks
draft them and Jacob signs them. Nothing they write goes to a customer,
and nothing gets called *shipped* or *cited*, without a human hand on it.
That's the deal in writing, in this repo, and it doesn't change.
